Hosting on Udala works the same way whether you're planning a low-key meetup or a ticketed party: you create the event, people RSVP as Going, Interested, or Can't Go, and you manage the whole thing from **Events → Manage**. A few things make the difference between an event that fills up and one that quietly doesn't.

## Get the basics right

Members are deciding whether to RSVP in seconds, so the fields that show up first matter most:

- **Title and event type** should tell someone exactly what kind of night this is before they read another word.
- **A real cover image** outperforms a blank placeholder every time — it's the first thing anyone sees in the Events grid.
- **Venue and address** should be as specific as you're comfortable sharing; vague locations quietly kill RSVPs.
- **Price**, if any, should be set upfront. Surprise costs at the door are the fastest way to lose trust.

If you want more control over who shows up, mark the event **Private** — RSVPs still work the same way, but the event won't appear in public browsing.

## Read your "Who's going?" breakdown

Every event page shows a live breakdown of who's RSVP'd — by couples, singles, creators, and new members — plus a count of people you already follow who are attending. Use it. If you're seeing mostly "Interested" and few "Going," that's usually a sign the date, price, or description needs adjusting rather than a sign to just wait it out.

## Priced events and payments

If you set a price, RSVPing "Going" walks the attendee through checkout before their spot is confirmed — nobody occupies a paid slot without actually paying for it. You can track transactions and attendee status from the manage page at any time.

## Chat before the event

Every event gets its own group chat, open to you and anyone who's RSVP'd "Going." Use it to send reminders, answer logistics questions, or just build a bit of anticipation before the day arrives. As host, you can moderate the chat directly — mute anyone who's disruptive or remove a message — without having to leave the page.

## After the event

Attendees who shared the event with you become eligible to leave you a review once it's over, and vice versa. Encourage people to do it — reviews are one of the clearest signals new members use to decide whether to RSVP to your next one.

## The short version

Specific beats vague, a real cover image beats a placeholder, and price transparency beats a discount announced at the door. Everything else is refinement.
