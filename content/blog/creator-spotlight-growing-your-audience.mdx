Creator tools on Udala exist so you can build something sustainable: tiered subscriptions, private-access and VIP circles, and a dashboard that tells you what's actually working instead of leaving you to guess. Here's how creators who are growing tend to use them.

## Structure your tiers around actual differences

The most common mistake is pricing tiers by how much content you post rather than by what a subscriber actually gets. Instead, think about it as different *relationships* with your audience:

- A **lower-priced base tier** for people who want ongoing access to your regular posts.
- A **mid-tier or VIP circle** for a smaller, more engaged group — think extra posts, faster replies, or a private circle feed.
- A **limited private-access tier**, capped at a small number of subscribers, for your most invested fans.

You can mark any tier as limited or requiring approval from **Creator Studio → Tiers**, which also lets you see subscriber counts at a glance.

## Post on a rhythm your audience can feel

Content that goes out on some recognizable rhythm outperforms a burst of posts followed by silence — subscribers stick around for creators who feel reliably present, even if the frequency itself is modest. Your dashboard's **Assistant** tab looks at when your subscribers are actually active and suggests posting windows based on that activity, so you're not guessing at timing.

## Watch your dashboard, not just your notifications

The **Creator Studio** dashboard gives you a few things worth checking regularly:

- **Subscriber growth** over time, so you can see which changes (a new tier, a pricing change, a specific post) actually moved the needle.
- **Earnings by month**, broken out clearly enough to spot trends before they become problems.
- **Applications**, for any tier that requires your approval — don't let these sit unanswered.
- **Assistant insights**, including your top fans, subscribers who've gone quiet, and ready-to-send quick replies for common situations like welcoming a new subscriber or re-engaging someone who hasn't been active in a while.

## Use verification to build trust

Applying for **Verified creator** status from your dashboard signals to potential subscribers that a real person is behind the account, reviewed manually rather than self-declared. It's optional, but it's one of the simplest trust signals you can add with no ongoing effort once it's approved.

## The short version

Price around relationships, not volume. Post on a rhythm. Check your dashboard before you post your next thing, not just after. And if you haven't applied for verification yet, it's a five-minute upload that pays off in trust.
