## Assistant

This is genuinely a strong acquisition idea for Udala. It has curiosity, identity, surprise and shareability built into it:

> "We can guess your spec—and explain why you're attracted to them."

The psychologically credible version would not claim to identify one exact body type or appearance. It would uncover the person's broader attraction pattern: the appearance, energy, behaviour and emotional experience they repeatedly find compelling.

One important truth makes the quiz even more interesting: people are often poor at describing what actually attracts them. In speed-dating research, stated preferences did not reliably predict attraction to people participants met, although longer-term studies suggest preferences have some predictive value. In other words, people have a "declared spec" and a partly unconscious "revealed spec." [Eastwick & Finkel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18211175/), [Gerlach et al. study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28921999/)

## What influences someone's "spec"?

| Influence | What it can shape | What you could observe indirectly |
|---|---|---|
| Familiarity | Attraction to faces, styles and personalities that feel recognisable | Childhood environment, early crushes, celebrities, repeated social exposure |
| Similarity | Preference for people with comparable values, education, humour, religion or lifestyle | Their ideal weekend, ambitions, social world and beliefs |
| Aspirational identity | Attraction to someone who represents who they want to become | The lifestyles, rooms, clothes and social scenes they admire |
| Emotional needs | Whether they prefer comforting, attentive people or exciting, challenging people | What makes them feel wanted, safe, intrigued or alive |
| Social energy | Preference for quiet confidence, popularity, boldness, mystery or playfulness | Who they notice at parties and how they like someone to approach them |
| Sensory attraction | Whether chemistry begins through appearance, voice, scent, movement or conversation | What they remember first after meeting an attractive stranger |
| Relationship intention | Different preferences when imagining casual chemistry versus a serious relationship | Their ideal pace, level of commitment, spontaneity and emotional depth |
| Previous reinforcement | Traits associated with memorable crushes and relationships can become more attractive—or become turn-offs | Patterns in people they repeatedly choose, even when those choices do not work |
| Culture and media | Local beauty standards, class signals, fashion, celebrity culture and gender expectations | Entertainment tastes, admired public figures and aesthetic preferences |
| Self-perception | What people think they can attract and what kind of partner validates them | Which compliments affect them and whose attention makes them feel special |

People also tend to pair with others who resemble them across several demographic, behavioural and value dimensions, although the reasons include social environment and available dating pools—not just innate attraction. [Review of assortative mating research](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/spc3.12337)

Repeated exposure can increase liking up to a point, which helps explain why familiar "types" may become appealing without people consciously deciding on them. [Mere-exposure meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000085)

## The quiz should calculate an attraction blueprint

Instead of assigning everyone one simplistic label, score them across six underlying dimensions:

1. **Warmth ↔ Edge**  
   Nurturing and reassuring versus challenging and unpredictable.

2. **Stability ↔ Adventure**  
   Consistent and grounded versus spontaneous and novelty-seeking.

3. **Understated ↔ Expressive**  
   Quiet confidence versus visible charisma.

4. **Depth ↔ Status**  
   Emotional or intellectual connection versus ambition, polish and social presence.

5. **Familiarity ↔ Contrast**  
   Someone similar to them versus someone who introduces a different world.

6. **Slow burn ↔ Instant spark**  
   Attraction that develops through familiarity versus immediate physical chemistry.

You can add secondary scores for humour, ambition, sensuality, intelligence, attentiveness, independence and preferred social lifestyle.

The result would combine their strongest dimensions into a recognisable "spec."

## Possible result types

- **The Quiet Fire** — composed, private, observant and surprisingly intense.
- **The Soft Landing** — affectionate, emotionally available and reassuring.
- **The Electric Charmer** — bold, playful, sociable and difficult to ignore.
- **The Ambitious Icon** — polished, driven, respected and visibly going somewhere.
- **The Brilliant Tease** — intelligent, witty and mentally stimulating.
- **The Beautiful Mystery** — stylish, selective, restrained and slightly difficult to read.
- **The Free Spirit** — expressive, adventurous and resistant to predictability.
- **The Grounded Equal** — dependable, values-driven and naturally compatible with their life.

Each result should contain:

- The kind of person who catches their attention
- The behaviour that creates attraction
- The visual or social energy they notice
- What this may suggest about their personality
- What they secretly want to feel around someone
- Their attraction blind spot
- The difference between who excites them and who suits them
- How they flirt when genuinely interested
- A Udala call to action

For example:

> **Your spec is The Quiet Fire.**  
> You're drawn to people who appear calm but carry a strong presence. They do not compete for everybody's attention, which makes their attention feel more valuable when they give it to you. You likely value selectiveness, depth and emotional control. Your blind spot is occasionally mistaking distance for mystery. The person who suits you best combines quiet confidence with genuine consistency.

That feels like a reading, rather than merely saying, "You like introverts."

## Fun questions with psychological meaning underneath

The questions should appear to be about moments, moods and choices—not clinical personality statements.

### 1. You arrive at a rooftop party. Who do you notice first?

A. The person making everyone laugh  
B. The person quietly watching everything  
C. The person who seems to know everybody  
D. The person helping the host sort something out

This measures playfulness, mystery, social status and warmth.

### 2. Which movie moment creates the most chemistry?

A. Two people teasing each other while cooking  
B. A long look across a crowded room  
C. A perfectly dressed couple making an entrance  
D. A vulnerable conversation at 2 a.m.

This measures humour, tension, aspiration and emotional intimacy.

### 3. Someone likes you. Which move is most likely to work?

A. They confidently ask you out  
B. They remember an insignificant detail you mentioned  
C. They send something that makes you laugh unexpectedly  
D. They give you space but remain consistent

This measures initiative, attentiveness, playfulness and secure independence.

### 4. Which late-night message would tempt you most?

A. "I'm downstairs. Come out."  
B. A thoughtful three-minute voice note  
C. A meme that only the two of you would understand  
D. "I made reservations. Be ready at eight tomorrow."

This separates spontaneity, emotional depth, shared humour and deliberate effort.

### 5. Pick the couple's Sunday you would secretly want.

A. Brunch, an event and meeting people  
B. Phones off, food ordered and nowhere to go  
C. An unplanned trip outside the city  
D. Working toward a shared goal together

This detects social lifestyle, closeness, novelty and ambition.

### 6. Which compliment stays with you longest?

A. "You look incredible."  
B. "I feel peaceful around you."  
C. "The way your mind works is attractive."  
D. "There's something about your energy."

This reveals the form of validation that matters most to them.

### 7. Which flaw can instantly ruin attraction?

A. Neediness  
B. Arrogance  
C. Predictability  
D. Lack of ambition

Turn-offs are often as informative as desired characteristics.

### 8. Choose a home for one unforgettable weekend.

A. A polished penthouse overlooking the city  
B. A quiet cabin surrounded by trees  
C. A colourful apartment in the centre of everything  
D. A secluded beach house with no neighbours

This indirectly measures status, intimacy, stimulation and privacy.

### 9. Someone attractive disagrees with you. What makes it hotter rather than annoying?

A. They calmly defend their position  
B. They turn it into playful banter  
C. They genuinely try to understand you  
D. They confidently challenge your assumptions

This measures composure, humour, empathy and intellectual dominance.

### 10. What do you usually remember first about someone?

A. Their face and clothes  
B. Their voice and manner of speaking  
C. How they moved and occupied the room  
D. How they made you feel

This helps identify their dominant channel of attraction.

The user does not need to know what every individual answer measures. However, Udala should clearly disclose that their answers are being used to generate a personalised attraction result. The scoring can be hidden; the fact that you are analysing their answers should not be.

## Recommended MVP

Build a short version first:

- 18+ age confirmation
- 10 scenario questions
- 4 image-choice questions
- 2 intelligent tie-breakers
- Approximately four minutes
- One primary spec and two secondary traits
- Instant short result
- Optional email or WhatsApp entry to save the full reading
- A shareable result card
- CTA: **"Meet people who match your energy on Udala."**

Do not advertise it as a scientifically validated psychological test unless it has actually been validated. Call it:

> "A playful, research-informed reading of your attraction pattern."

Because sexual and romantic preferences are sensitive, users should also be able to take the quiz without Udala quietly using their answers for advertising or exposing them publicly.

## Your spec: The Quiet Fire

You're attracted to people who don't have to fight for attention to command it. They may be reserved, observant or slightly difficult to read—but underneath that calm exterior, you sense confidence, intelligence and intensity.

### What your spec says about you

You don't give everyone access to you, even when you appear friendly. You observe people closely: how they treat others, how they carry themselves and whether their confidence is genuine or performed. Loud attraction can entertain you, but subtle chemistry stays in your mind much longer.

You probably enjoy discovering people gradually. A look that lasts a second too long, a conversation with an unexpected layer or the feeling that someone behaves differently around you can affect you more than obvious flirting.

You want to feel chosen—not merely included among twenty people receiving the same attention.

### Your dating pattern

You may take time to admit that you like someone. By the time other people notice your interest, you've probably already replayed several interactions and noticed details the person doesn't realise they revealed.

You're often attracted to people who seem emotionally controlled. Their restraint creates space for your imagination, and uncertainty can make the attraction stronger. Once you feel safe, however, you're much warmer, more playful and more emotionally invested than your initial behaviour suggests.

### Your blind spot

You can occasionally mistake emotional unavailability for mystery. Because you're attracted to what must be uncovered, someone being inconsistent may initially feel intriguing rather than frustrating.

But mystery and confusion are not the same thing. Your best match has depth without making you beg for clarity.

### What actually works for you

You need someone calm but intentional—someone who gives you room while making their interest unmistakable. They don't need to tell the whole world how they feel, but they must make sure you know.

**Your attraction truth:** You don't fall for the loudest person in the room. You fall for the person who makes the room disappear.

## Your spec: The Soft Landing

You're drawn to warmth: the kind of person whose presence makes you unclench without realising you were tense. They are attentive, affectionate and emotionally generous. Around them, you feel accepted rather than assessed.

### What your spec says about you

Despite whatever independent image you present, emotional safety matters deeply to you. You want attraction, but you also want relief—the freedom to be imperfect, playful, tired or vulnerable without feeling that affection will suddenly be withdrawn.

Small acts affect you more than extravagant performances. Remembering what you said, checking that you arrived safely, noticing a change in your mood or bringing you something you like can mean more than an expensive but impersonal gesture.

You probably remember how people made you feel long after forgetting exactly what they said.

### Your dating pattern

You may initially claim that you're "just seeing how things go," but once somebody makes you feel understood, you begin imagining how naturally they could fit into your life.

You give a lot when you care. You notice needs, offer reassurance and often become the person others rely on. Because of this, you're particularly affected by relationships where your tenderness is enjoyed but not returned.

You don't necessarily require constant communication. What you require is emotional consistency: you want to know that today's affection will not become tomorrow's unexplained distance.

### Your blind spot

You can sometimes confuse being needed with being loved. A person's vulnerability may activate your caring side before they have demonstrated that they can care for you equally.

Be careful of becoming someone's emotional home while remaining a visitor in theirs.

### What actually works for you

Your best match is affectionate without being dependent, reassuring without becoming possessive and emotionally open without turning you into their therapist.

**Your attraction truth:** The quickest way to your heart is not impressing you. It is making you feel safe enough to put your guard down.

## Your spec: The Electric Charmer

You like presence. Your spec is expressive, confident, playful and socially alive—the kind of person who walks into a room and changes its temperature.

### What your spec says about you

You're energised by chemistry you can feel immediately. You enjoy people who give you something to respond to: banter, boldness, eye contact, spontaneity and a little unpredictability.

Even if you're naturally quiet, you may be attracted to people who draw out the livelier side of you. If you're already outgoing, you want someone capable of matching your energy rather than merely watching it.

You don't just want to admire someone. You want an experience with them.

### Your dating pattern

Your strongest connections may begin quickly. There's usually a memorable first interaction, an unusually easy conversation or a moment when both of you recognise the tension.

When interested, you become more playful. You tease, create inside jokes and test whether the other person can keep up. A person can be conventionally attractive and still lose you if interacting with them feels flat.

You're likely to value stories, memorable dates and relationships with a sense of movement. Too much routine without emotional or playful energy can make you wonder whether the spark has disappeared.

### Your blind spot

You can overestimate compatibility when chemistry is intense. Charismatic people are often skilled at creating exciting moments, but excitement does not automatically indicate consistency, empathy or genuine intention.

Sometimes the person who gives you butterflies is activating your uncertainty, not revealing your soulmate.

### What actually works for you

You need someone vibrant who can also become steady when it matters. The right person doesn't extinguish the excitement; they prove that attraction can remain alive without keeping you anxious.

**Your attraction truth:** You fall for energy first—but the person who keeps you must bring substance after the spark.

## Your spec: The Ambitious Icon

You're attracted to people who appear purposeful. They're polished, capable and visibly building something—a career, a business, a reputation or simply a life that reflects high standards.

### What your spec says about you

You respect effort. Appearance catches your eye, but what truly impresses you is the sense that somebody has direction. You want to admire the person you're dating, and you probably want them to be equally proud of you.

You may be attracted to confidence because you value competence in yourself. Even if you haven't reached every personal goal, you think about the future and dislike the idea of being tied to somebody who has stopped growing.

For you, attraction and respect are closely connected.

### Your dating pattern

You notice presentation: clothes, speech, manners, ambition and how someone behaves in important settings. You don't necessarily need wealth, but you want evidence of discipline and potential.

You probably enjoy deliberate gestures. A properly planned date can impress you more than a vague invitation because planning communicates that your time matters.

Once committed, you may naturally think in terms of partnership: what could we build, experience or accomplish together?

### Your blind spot

You can sometimes be dazzled by the appearance of success. Status, confidence and polish can conceal emotional immaturity just as easily as they can signal genuine competence.

You may also dismiss promising people too quickly if they are still becoming who they intend to be. Direction can matter more than current position.

### What actually works for you

Your ideal person is driven but emotionally present. They have their own life, but they don't treat affection like another appointment squeezed into their calendar.

**Your attraction truth:** You don't simply want someone attractive. You want someone whose life makes you think, "Yes, this person makes sense beside me."

## Your spec: The Brilliant Tease

Your attraction often begins in your mind. You're drawn to intelligence, humour and verbal chemistry—the person who catches your meaning quickly, challenges you playfully and knows exactly when to say something outrageous.

### What your spec says about you

Being understood is seductive to you. You want somebody who notices the layers beneath your words and gives you conversations that don't feel interchangeable with everyone else's.

Humour is especially important because it reveals speed, confidence and compatibility simultaneously. When someone understands your jokes—and makes references only you would appreciate—it creates a private little world between you.

You probably develop attraction through conversation more often than you admit. Someone may become significantly better-looking to you once you discover how their mind works.

### Your dating pattern

When you like somebody, you may flirt by teasing them, debating unnecessary topics or sending oddly specific content. Your affection can hide inside jokes, mock arguments and conversations that continue much longer than either person intended.

You lose interest when communication becomes repetitive. "Have you eaten?" may be sweet, but you need more than daily attendance. You want curiosity, surprise and the feeling that the other person is genuinely engaging with you.

### Your blind spot

You can mistake conversational brilliance for emotional intelligence. Someone may know how to stimulate your mind while remaining evasive about their feelings.

You may also use humour to avoid revealing that something actually matters to you. If everything becomes a joke, the other person may never realise when you need sincerity.

### What actually works for you

You need someone clever enough to intrigue you and emotionally mature enough to speak plainly when the jokes end.

**Your attraction truth:** Your real foreplay is the conversation that makes you look at the time and realise it is suddenly 2 a.m.

### What actually works for you

Your best relationship contains privacy, individuality and continual discovery, alongside honest communication. You need someone who keeps some mystery without keeping you insecure.

**Your attraction truth:** You're rarely captivated by what everyone can see. You want to discover the version of someone that not everyone gets.

You're attracted to people who feel alive. They're expressive, adventurous and comfortable doing things differently. Being around them makes life appear larger and less predictable.

### What your spec says about you

Freedom matters to you, even if you also want closeness. You dislike relationships that immediately begin to feel like obligations, routines or a list of rules. You want to choose someone repeatedly, not feel trapped into performing romance.

You may be attracted to people who embody qualities you want more of in yourself: courage, spontaneity, creativity or the ability to ignore unnecessary judgment.

Your ideal relationship feels like both a connection and an adventure.

### Your dating pattern

You're probably drawn to unusual stories, distinctive personalities and people who introduce you to something new. Dates become more exciting when they contain surprise, movement or a little harmless chaos.

You need space to remain an individual, and you are likely to give your partner similar freedom—provided trust has been established.

### Your blind spot

You can mistake inconsistency for spontaneity. Someone constantly changing plans, avoiding responsibility or appearing only when it suits them is not necessarily free-spirited; they may simply be unreliable.

You may also retreat when a relationship becomes serious because stability initially feels like a loss of excitement.

### What actually works for you

You need someone open-minded and adventurous who still keeps their word. The right relationship expands your world without destabilising it.

**Your attraction truth:** You want someone who makes you forget the plan—not someone who makes you regret having no plan.

### What actually works for you

You need someone dependable who still brings curiosity, affection and intentional romance into everyday life. Compatibility should be the foundation—not the excuse to stop trying.

**Your attraction truth:** You're not looking for someone who turns your entire life upside down. You want someone who makes your existing life feel better to live.
