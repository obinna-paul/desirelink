# Udala Spec Test: Psychological Framework, Quiz Design, Scoring and Interpretation

## Executive summary

The current eight Spec Types are good brand names but should not be treated as a scientifically established number. They describe attractive character sketches, yet they do not distinguish four separate questions:

1. Who captures a person's attention?
2. What makes that person feel chosen and emotionally safe?
3. What pattern does the person repeat when attraction becomes uncertain?
4. What qualities actually support a satisfying long-term relationship?

Those answers often differ. Someone may be magnetized by restraint and ambiguity, need considerable reassurance once attached, and function best with a calm, direct partner. Calling that person simply “Quiet Fire” misses the psychologically interesting part. The revealing insight is the tension: *you are drawn to what makes you lean in, but you stay well with someone who does not make you keep guessing.*

The recommended system uses **continuous attraction motives** underneath and consumer-friendly archetypes on top. The number of motives and the number of archetypes are separate questions. Theory supports a provisional seven-motive model; the final number of public-facing Specs should be selected from pilot data rather than fixed at eight. Every respondent receives:

- a primary Spec motive;
- a secondary Spec influence;
- a seven-motive attraction profile, revised if validation supports a different structure;
- a Spark–Safety relationship tension;
- an attachment-response lens;
- an intimacy pace and relationship-orientation lens;
- conditional observations about likely dating patterns;
- a long-term partner brief framed as guidance, not destiny;
- a confidence label that admits when the evidence supports a blend rather than a single type.

The eight existing names can remain as a **provisional narrative library** while the model is tested. They are best understood as combinations of deeper motives:

| Provisional Spec | Dominant motive pattern | What the person is really seeking |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet Fire | contained depth | intensity that feels earned rather than advertised |
| Soft Landing | responsive safety | affection, attunement and the freedom to exhale |
| Electric Charmer | social vitality | aliveness, immediacy and mutual momentum |
| Ambitious Icon | admired agency | competence, direction and a partner worthy of respect |
| Brilliant Tease | mental attunement | wit, curiosity and the pleasure of being mentally met |
| Beautiful Mystery | aesthetic intrigue | self-possession, selectivity and gradual discovery |
| Free Spirit | self-expansion | novelty, autonomy and a life that becomes larger together |
| Grounded Equal | reciprocal partnership | reliability, shared values and love that works in ordinary life |

This structure is informed by research on ideal-partner standards, attachment, personality traits, sociosexual orientation, interpersonal agency and communion, partner responsiveness, self-expansion, and psychometric test design. It should be marketed as a **research-informed reading**, not a clinical psychological test. Until Udala validates the instrument against real behavior, “We can guess your Spec” is an appropriate playful promise; “we scientifically know your exact type” is not.

### Recommendation on the number of motives and types

No published theory establishes that attraction naturally divides into eight motives. The evidence provides several recurring domains, but the correct dimensionality for Udala's audience is an empirical question. The recommended starting hypothesis is **seven continuous motives**:

1. warmth and responsiveness;
2. reliability and reciprocity;
3. social and embodied vitality;
4. agency, ambition and direction;
5. cognitive play and mental attunement;
6. novelty, autonomy and self-expansion;
7. intrigue, restraint and selective access, with depth/privacy and aesthetic/selectivity tested as separate facets.

Seven is a research plan, not a foregone conclusion. Pilot analysis should compare plausible five- through nine-factor models. The consumer-facing taxonomy should then compare roughly six through twelve profile solutions and select the smallest set that is statistically stable, psychologically distinct, narratively useful and reproducible in a hold-out sample. Eight may survive; it may also become six, seven, nine or ten.

## 1. The book and popular personality systems

### The book is Tim LaHaye's *Why You Act the Way You Do*

The remembered title and author are correct: *Why You Act the Way You Do* by Tim LaHaye. The book applies the four classical temperaments—sanguine, choleric, melancholic and phlegmatic—and blends them into additional profiles. It explicitly discusses strengths, weaknesses, sexuality, marriage and spouse selection. It is also a practical, Christian, pastoral work rather than a modern psychometrically validated model of personality.[^1]

The book is useful for **narrative technique**. Its profiles feel substantial because they connect temperament to work, emotion, conflict, love, blind spots and growth. Udala should borrow that breadth and human tone. It should not adopt the four temperaments as the scoring foundation.

### MBTI is useful language, but not the right engine

The initials being recalled are probably types such as **INFP, ENFJ, INTP or ESFJ** from the Myers–Briggs Type Indicator. MBTI is memorable because it gives people a compact identity and a community of similar types. However, it forces continuous tendencies into binary categories and is not designed to measure romantic attraction. Research has shown that much of its useful variance can be reinterpreted through broader trait models such as the Five-Factor Model; even sympathetic uses of MBTI require more caution than casual online typing usually provides.[^2]

Udala can borrow three product lessons from MBTI:

- a memorable label gives people language for themselves;
- a secondary type makes the result feel less reductive;
- differences should be described without calling one type superior.

Udala should not use MBTI letters, imply MBTI compatibility, copy proprietary items, or calculate a Spec from a user's MBTI type.

### Big Five and HEXACO are stronger foundations—but still incomplete

The Big Five treats personality as continuous scores in openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and negative emotionality. The BFI-2 has a hierarchical structure with five domains and 15 facets and has substantial validation evidence.[^3] A meta-analysis also linked several Big Five traits—especially lower neuroticism and higher agreeableness and conscientiousness—to relationship satisfaction, although much of that evidence came from heterosexual samples and does not turn personality into a compatibility oracle.[^4]

HEXACO adds Honesty–Humility, which is conceptually useful for distinguishing polish or charisma from sincerity and non-exploitation. Neither model, however, directly answers “what energy attracts you?” The Spec Test should use a few trait-like indicators as **interpretive lenses**, not attempt a full Big Five or HEXACO assessment in four minutes.

### Zodiac signs should not enter the psychological score

Astrology can be culturally enjoyable, but the evidence does not justify using zodiac signs as predictors of personality or partner compatibility. A large study of more than 15,000 people found no relationship between date of birth and personality or general intelligence and no support for astrological sun-sign associations.[^5] Classic double-blind work likewise failed to support natal-chart matching beyond chance.[^6]

There is also a product risk: vague flattering descriptions can feel unusually accurate even when everyone receives the same text. This is the Barnum–Forer effect, demonstrated when participants strongly endorsed identical “personalized” profiles.[^7] A result feeling accurate is therefore not, by itself, proof that the scoring works.

**Recommendation:** birthday may be requested optionally for age eligibility, celebratory UX, or a clearly labeled “cosmic side note.” It should contribute **zero weight** to the psychological formula. A playful line could say: *“Your sign adds the drama. Your answers did the scoring.”*

## 2. What psychology can genuinely contribute

### 2.1 Ideal-partner standards

The Ideal Standards Model found that partner ideals cluster around three broad dimensions: **warmth/trustworthiness, vitality/attractiveness and status/resources**.[^8] These provide a defensible foundation for Soft Landing and Grounded Equal, Electric Charmer and Beautiful Mystery, and Ambitious Icon respectively.

Yet ideals are not perfect forecasts of desire. Speed-dating work has repeatedly shown gaps between what people say they value and whom they desire after meeting. More recent multinational evidence suggests ideal-preference matching can predict some outcomes, especially when preferences are measured and analyzed carefully, but effects are not strong enough to identify a destined partner.[^9] This is why the quiz needs indirect trade-offs and scenarios, not only “What do you want in a partner?”

### 2.2 Attachment is a response pattern, not a romantic destiny

Adult attachment is most usefully measured on two continuous dimensions:

- **attachment anxiety:** sensitivity to rejection, abandonment and inconsistent reassurance;
- **attachment avoidance:** discomfort with dependence, vulnerability or excessive closeness.

A large meta-analysis covering 132 studies and more than 71,000 people found that both anxiety and avoidance were associated with lower relationship satisfaction, with stronger effects for a person's own experience than their partner's.[^10] Attachment should therefore help explain *what happens after attraction begins*. It should not be turned into four dramatic identity labels or used to diagnose users.

### 2.3 Responsiveness matters to both security and desire

Perceived partner responsiveness means feeling understood, validated and cared for. It is not merely “niceness.” Research using experimental, observational and daily-experience methods found responsiveness associated with sexual desire, partly because it made people feel valued and their partner seem like a desirable mate.[^11] This gives Soft Landing more depth and prevents warmth from being framed as the boring opposite of chemistry.

### 2.4 Novelty and expansion are legitimate attraction motives

Self-expansion theory proposes that people are drawn to relationships that enlarge their experiences, identities and capabilities. Experimental and survey research found that couples participating in novel, arousing activities reported increases in relationship quality compared with mundane activities.[^12] This supports Free Spirit, but the test must separate healthy expansion from unreliability. “Adventurous” and “inconsistent” are not synonyms.

### 2.5 Intimacy, passion and commitment are separate—even when related

Sternberg's framework distinguishes intimacy, passion and commitment. A recent 15-item version was validated across 37 languages and a sample spanning 156 countries, while also finding the dimensions highly related.[^13] Udala does not need to administer that instrument. It should use the distinction to avoid assuming that a person who creates passion automatically supplies intimacy or commitment.

### 2.6 Sociosexual orientation changes what “my type” means

Sociosexuality concerns comfort with sex outside committed relationships. The SOI-R separates **behavior, attitude and desire** rather than treating them as one thing.[^14] For an adult, sex-positive platform, this matters: two users may share an attraction type but want completely different structures around that attraction.

Ask only what the result genuinely needs. Relationship intent and preferred pace can usually provide enough interpretation for the free quiz. Explicit sexual-history questions should be optional, age-gated and separately consented to.

### 2.7 Long-term quality cannot be inferred from “type” alone

Across 43 longitudinal couples datasets, machine-learning analyses found relationship-specific perceptions—such as perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, partner satisfaction and conflict—to be among the strongest self-report predictors of relationship quality. Individual traits and partner reports added much less predictive power.[^15]

That finding should shape the marriage guidance. The test can describe the **qualities and interaction patterns** likely to suit the respondent; it should not claim that one archetype is the person they must marry.

## 3. The recommended model: the Spec Compass

The product should score continuous motives underneath and generate shareable archetypes from recurrent combinations. A type is a recognizable region in attraction space, not a box a person permanently inhabits.

### Layer A: seven candidate attraction motives

| Code | Motive | High-score signal | It is not automatically |
|---|---|---|---|
| W | Warmth & Responsiveness | tenderness, reassurance, emotional presence | dependence or passivity |
| R | Reliability & Reciprocity | follow-through, equality, shared values, low drama | blandness or lack of desire |
| V | Social & Embodied Vitality | boldness, play, flirtation, expressive presence | immaturity or promiscuity |
| A | Agency & Direction | ambition, polish, competence, status and purpose | wealth, domination or vanity |
| C | Cognitive Play | wit, intelligence, verbal play and curiosity | credentials or cold rationality |
| N | Novelty & Autonomy | spontaneity, self-expansion, expression and freedom | chaos or fear of commitment |
| I | Intrigue & Selective Access | restraint, privacy, style, depth and gradual reveal | manipulation or emotional unavailability |

The seventh motive deliberately contains two candidate facets—**contained depth/privacy** and **aesthetic selectivity**. Pilot data may show that these are one broader intrigue factor, two separate dimensions, or merely different surface expressions of other motives. This is the principal reason not to freeze the taxonomy at eight.

### Layer A2: archetypes as motive combinations

The current names become hypotheses about profile shapes:

| Provisional archetype | Expected motive shape |
|---|---|
| Quiet Fire | high I-depth, moderate W/C, low public V |
| Soft Landing | high W, moderate R, low tolerance for ambiguity |
| Electric Charmer | high V, often C, fast spark |
| Ambitious Icon | high A, often R or V depending on subtype |
| Brilliant Tease | high C, often V or I-depth |
| Beautiful Mystery | high I-aesthetic, often A, selective/slow reveal |
| Free Spirit | high N, often V, strong autonomy |
| Grounded Equal | high R and W, strong mutuality |

If two profiles cannot be distinguished reliably, merge them. If one label repeatedly contains two internally different groups, split it. If a large stable profile has no suitable name, create a new Spec.

### Layer B: eight interpretive lenses

These lenses explain *how* a person experiences their type:

1. **Spark–Safety balance:** arousal and immediacy versus calm and predictability.
2. **Closeness–Autonomy:** desire for togetherness versus protected independence.
3. **Fast–Slow burn:** speed of emotional and physical investment.
4. **Directness–Intrigue:** preference for clarity versus gradual discovery.
5. **Private–Public expression:** quiet intimacy versus visible affection and social display.
6. **Admiration–Mutuality:** looking up to a partner versus feeling side-by-side.
7. **Mind–Embodied energy:** verbal/cognitive chemistry versus presence, appearance and social energy.
8. **Exploration–Commitment:** openness to casual discovery versus commitment-first orientation.

### Layer C: attachment-response lens

Use a small number of original scenario items to estimate low/moderate/high tendencies toward anxiety and avoidance. Do not report “secure,” “anxious,” “avoidant” or “fearful” as diagnoses. Translate the score into ordinary language:

- **Steady under uncertainty:** does not strongly escalate or withdraw.
- **Reassurance-sensitive:** ambiguity increases attention and a need for confirmation.
- **Space-protective:** pressure increases withdrawal and self-reliance.
- **Push–pull:** both longing and retreat can appear when stakes rise.

### Layer D: relationship context

Ask whether the respondent is single, dating, partnered or married; what relationship structure they are open to; and what they currently want. These answers should adjust advice, not the core attraction score. Someone seeking casual connection can still be a Soft Landing; someone seeking marriage can still be an Electric Charmer.

## 4. Provisional narrative library for the current eight Specs

The following are strong base narratives for the current concept set, not a recommendation that the final product must contain exactly eight types. In production, each should be combined with the respondent's secondary profile and lenses. Statements about past behavior must appear only when the relevant pattern flags are present.

### 4.1 Quiet Fire (`quiet_fire`)

**Core reading.** You notice the person who does not spend the whole room trying to be noticed. Their restraint reads as self-command; their privacy suggests there is more to discover. You are not attracted to emptiness. You are attracted to *compression*—emotion, desire or intelligence that seems powerful precisely because it is not spilling everywhere.

**What this may say about you.** You are sensitive to small signals: a held gaze, a precise sentence, the difference between shyness and confidence. Loud performance can make you suspicious. You want access that feels earned, because earned access feels more meaningful than attention freely distributed to everyone.

**Likely strength.** You can appreciate subtle people without demanding constant performance. You often recognize depth others overlook and may be capable of loyalty that is quiet but intense.

**Dating-history module, only if ambiguity and anxiety are also high.** You may recognize a pattern in which the least available person becomes the most mentally present. A delayed reply creates more thought than a clear answer; rare tenderness feels disproportionately powerful. The attraction is real, but uncertainty may be amplifying it.

**Blind spot.** You can mistake emotional opacity for emotional depth. Some people are private because they are discerning; others are simply unavailable, conflict-avoidant or unwilling to communicate. Mystery should eventually become knowledge.

**Long-term partner brief.** The strongest fit is self-possessed and emotionally consistent: someone who respects privacy without making intimacy a guessing game, reveals themselves gradually but answers direct questions, and can hold intensity without using silence as power.

**Growth line.** Do not ask only, “How strongly do I feel?” Ask, “What have they consistently shown me?”

### 4.2 Soft Landing (`soft_landing`)

**Core reading.** Your attraction system relaxes when care is unmistakable. You notice the person who remembers details, checks that you arrived safely, makes affection feel natural, and creates room for emotion without turning it into a problem to solve. For you, tenderness is not the consolation prize after chemistry; tenderness can be chemistry.

**What this may say about you.** You are highly responsive to emotional climate. You likely read tone, consistency and consideration as signs of seriousness. Even when you enjoy excitement, you do not want to audition indefinitely for a place in someone's life.

**Likely strength.** You understand that love is enacted in small, repeated moments. You are often capable of warmth, repair and reassurance, and you may help partners feel safe enough to become more honest.

**Dating-history module, only if caretaking and low boundaries are high.** You may have stayed because someone needed you, then realized that being indispensable is not the same as being cherished. You can become the translator, therapist or emotional project manager of a relationship.

**Blind spot.** Relief can masquerade as compatibility. After inconsistency, basic kindness can feel extraordinary. Warmth matters, but it must coexist with boundaries, accountability, desire and adult self-responsibility.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose warmth with backbone: affectionate, emotionally available, able to apologize, but capable of managing their own life. The right partner receives your care without making your care the entire structure holding them up.

**Growth line.** The safest love is not the one that needs you most; it is the one in which both people can give and receive.

### 4.3 Electric Charmer (`electric_charmer`)

**Core reading.** You feel attraction as momentum. The person who makes a dull room come alive, turns an ordinary exchange into flirtation, or creates a private joke within minutes has an advantage with you. You are drawn to social courage and emotional voltage—the feeling that something is already happening.

**What this may say about you.** Your nervous system notices energy before biography. You value presence, timing and reciprocal enthusiasm. You may also want proof that a partner can enter life fully rather than observing it from the edges.

**Likely strength.** You permit desire to be joyful. You can create play, initiate connection and prevent relationships from becoming purely administrative.

**Dating-history module, only if fast-burn and novelty are high.** Your stories may begin brilliantly: long first dates, instant inside jokes, a week that feels like a month. The harder part may arrive when charisma must become consistency and stimulation must survive routine.

**Blind spot.** Chemistry is information, not a full background check. Social fluency can look like intimacy because both create ease. The question is whether the person remains generous, accountable and interested when there is no audience and nothing exciting to win.

**Long-term partner brief.** The best fit has sparkle *and* follow-through: socially alive, sexually expressive and playful, but dependable when plans, conflict and ordinary responsibilities arrive.

**Growth line.** Do not extinguish the spark. Ask whether it is attached to a person who can keep a promise after the party ends.

### 4.4 Ambitious Icon (`ambitious_icon`)

**Core reading.** Respect is one of your gateways to desire. You notice competence, standards, direction and visible self-possession. It is not necessarily money that attracts you; it is evidence that a person can choose a path, carry weight and create a life rather than waiting for life to happen.

**What this may say about you.** Attraction and admiration are closely linked. You may take partnership seriously and imagine how two lives will fit in practice. A person's decisions, reputation and discipline can affect how physically attractive they become to you.

**Likely strength.** You see potential in terms of behavior, not only promises. You can build deliberately, support meaningful goals and respect a partner without diminishing yourself.

**Dating-history module, only if approval-seeking or comparison is high.** You may have pursued impressive people whose attention felt like confirmation of your own worth. A relationship could look enviable while leaving you emotionally underfed.

**Blind spot.** Competence in public does not guarantee generosity in private. Direction can become control; high standards can become chronic evaluation; polish can conceal an inability to repair harm.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose capable and emotionally generous. They should respect your goals, admire you back, share power, and know that success does not exempt them from tenderness, accountability or presence.

**Growth line.** The right person should be impressive to you, but you should not have to become an employee in their life to remain worthy of them.

### 4.5 Brilliant Tease (`brilliant_tease`)

**Core reading.** Your attraction often begins in language. A sharp observation, unexpected question or perfectly timed joke can alter a face for you. You are drawn to someone who can follow the leap in your mind, challenge you without flattening you, and make conversation feel like both play and discovery.

**What this may say about you.** Being mentally met is a form of intimacy. You probably dislike scripts, shallow praise and conversation that never develops texture. Humor may also help you test flexibility, confidence and whether another person can perceive subtext.

**Likely strength.** You can sustain curiosity and make a relationship intellectually alive. You tend to value individuality and may create unusually rich private languages with partners.

**Dating-history module, only if avoidance is elevated.** Banter may have carried relationships further than vulnerability did. You might know how to discuss everything except the one feeling that could change the conversation.

**Blind spot.** Verbal intelligence is not emotional intelligence. Wit can deflect, seduce, dominate or conceal contempt. Someone who understands your references may still fail to understand your needs.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose a lively mind with an accessible heart: curious, funny and articulate, but able to speak plainly, listen without preparing a comeback, and remain kind when humor is no longer enough.

**Growth line.** Notice who can make you laugh; choose among them by who can also make honesty feel safe.

### 4.6 Beautiful Mystery (`beautiful_mystery`)

**Core reading.** You are drawn to composition: style, restraint, taste, bearing and the sense that a person is not immediately available for mass interpretation. Their selectivity makes attention feel meaningful. Attraction grows through detail—the second look, the unexpected softness, the life behind a carefully held surface.

**What this may say about you.** You perceive aesthetics as information. Presentation can signal discernment, identity and self-respect. You may also have a strong imaginative life; what is not yet known creates room for possibility.

**Likely strength.** You appreciate beauty beyond conventional attractiveness and understand the erotic value of pacing. You may be good at allowing another person to unfold without forcing instant disclosure.

**Dating-history module, only if projection and uncertainty are high.** You may have fallen for the outline before meeting the whole person. Sparse information becomes a canvas; rare access feels like proof of value. Later, reality has to compete with a version your imagination helped create.

**Blind spot.** Selectivity is not character, and aesthetics can generate a halo around unrelated qualities. An elegant surface can coexist with evasiveness, vanity or poor treatment.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose intrigue that becomes intimacy. They can be stylish, independent and slow to reveal themselves, but should become clearer—not more confusing—as trust grows.

**Growth line.** Let beauty invite the first question. Let behavior answer the important ones.

### 4.7 Free Spirit (`free_spirit`)

**Core reading.** You want connection to enlarge life. You notice expressive people with stories, experiments, unusual interests and an ability to disrupt routine without demanding permission from the room. The attraction is not only to adventure; it is to possibility.

**What this may say about you.** Autonomy matters to your desire. You may need to feel that love opens doors rather than closes them. Predictability can feel safe at first and confining later if the relationship stops producing growth.

**Likely strength.** You bring flexibility, curiosity and reinvention. You can help a partner become less afraid of change and keep long-term love from shrinking into logistics.

**Dating-history module, only if consistency is low.** You may have interpreted intensity as freedom and routine as evidence that something was dying. Some endings may have occurred not because the connection was wrong, but because it became familiar before you learned how to create novelty together.

**Blind spot.** Unpredictability can imitate aliveness. A person who resists every structure may eventually make intimacy impossible, because trust needs enough continuity to accumulate.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose an adventurer with an anchor: open-minded, expressive and independent, but able to plan, repair, commit and support both shared and separate growth.

**Growth line.** Freedom in love is not the absence of promises; it is the ability to make promises that leave both people fully alive.

### 4.8 Grounded Equal (`grounded_equal`)

**Core reading.** You are attracted to evidence. The person does what they said, treats people consistently, contributes without scorekeeping, and can build a calm life without manufacturing emotional weather. Equality itself is attractive: neither rescuer nor project, neither fan nor authority.

**What this may say about you.** You understand that ordinary days are the majority of a relationship. Shared values, fairness and practical compatibility matter because you imagine not only dating someone but making decisions beside them.

**Likely strength.** You can distinguish attention from investment and drama from depth. You are well positioned to build trust through reciprocity and to approach conflict as a shared problem.

**Dating-history module, only if novelty is suppressed.** You may have chosen sensible relationships that worked on paper but did not make enough room for erotic or imaginative life. Stability can become avoidance when “we never fight” actually means “we never risk saying what we want.”

**Blind spot.** Low drama is healthy; low emotional range is not automatically healthy. Reliability does not remove the need for play, admiration, physical desire or surprise.

**Long-term partner brief.** Choose a true teammate with a pulse: dependable, fair and values-aligned, but capable of affection, initiative and enough novelty to keep partnership from becoming administration.

**Growth line.** You do not need chaos to create chemistry. You do need to keep choosing aliveness inside stability.

## 5. Quiz design

### Recommended duration and structure

Use **20 scored questions plus 3 optional context questions**. With concise options and one tap per item, median completion should be approximately four minutes. Ten questions are unlikely to support a stable multidimensional reading unless the quiz is adaptive and already has calibrated items.

| Section | Questions | Purpose | Weight in MVP |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| The Spark | 1–8 | indirect attraction and attention choices | 1.25 |
| The Pattern | 9–16 | reactions after interest forms | 1.00 |
| The Partnership | 17–20 | trade-offs relevant to durable fit | 1.15 |
| Context | 3 unscored/optional | relationship status, intent, birthday/age gate | interpretation only |

The introduction may say: *“Choose what feels true before you can make it sensible. There are no good answers—only revealing ones.”* This encourages spontaneous answers without falsely claiming that respondents do not know psychology is involved.

### Prototype 20-question item bank

The bracketed codes are internal candidate-motive keys and must never appear in the user interface. `I-depth` and `I-aesthetic` are deliberately retained as separate facets during piloting so the data can determine whether they belong together.

1. **At a crowded event, who makes you look twice?**
   - A. The quiet person clocking everything from the edge of the room. `[I-depth]`
   - B. The one turning strangers into friends within ten minutes. `[V]`
   - C. The polished person people instinctively ask for an opinion. `[A]`
   - D. The beautifully put-together person who leaves before anyone fully figures them out. `[I-aesthetic]`

2. **Which first date sounds most dangerous to your single era?**
   - A. Dinner where you somehow tell each other the truth. `[W]`
   - B. A bookstore, drinks, and a debate that becomes flirting. `[C]`
   - C. A spontaneous plan neither of you has tried before. `[N]`
   - D. A simple place, excellent conversation, and no performance. `[R]`

3. **Which message would replay in your head?**
   - A. “I don't say this often, but I feel unusually calm with you.” `[I-depth]`
   - B. “I remembered what you said yesterday. How did it go?” `[W]`
   - C. “You are annoyingly interesting. Continue.” `[C]`
   - D. A voice note that turns an ordinary Tuesday into an event. `[V]`

4. **Whose profile are you most likely to investigate?**
   - A. Clear goals, excellent work, very little noise. `[A]`
   - B. Immaculate taste and captions that reveal almost nothing. `[I-aesthetic]`
   - C. Different city every month; somehow every photo has a story. `[N]`
   - D. Friends, family, hobbies and an actual life they seem to like. `[R]`

5. **Which compliment would land deepest?**
   - A. “You change the energy of a room.” `[V]`
   - B. “I respect how you move through life.” `[A]`
   - C. “Talking to you rearranges how I think.” `[C]`
   - D. “I feel safe being fully myself with you.” `[W]`

6. **A pause falls in the conversation. Which version feels attractive?**
   - A. The silence feels charged, not awkward. `[I-depth]`
   - B. They hold your gaze like they know something you do not. `[I-aesthetic]`
   - C. They are comfortable enough not to fill every gap. `[R]`
   - D. They suddenly suggest going somewhere completely different. `[N]`

7. **Your person is hosting. What wins you over?**
   - A. They notice exactly who needs to be included. `[W]`
   - B. Everybody is laughing, but you still feel singled out. `[V]`
   - C. Everything runs beautifully without them looking stressed. `[A]`
   - D. The conversation at their table is the best one in the room. `[C]`

8. **Which slow reveal keeps you interested?**
   - A. They become warmer in private than anyone would expect. `[I-depth]`
   - B. Every meeting reveals another layer of taste and history. `[I-aesthetic]`
   - C. Their life keeps opening into experiences you never considered. `[N]`
   - D. Their consistency becomes more attractive the longer you watch. `[R]`

9. **A disagreement begins. Which response increases attraction?**
   - A. “I care about us. Let me understand what hurt.” `[W]`
   - B. They stay calm, decisive and willing to own their part. `[A]`
   - C. They can challenge your argument without attacking you. `[C]`
   - D. They focus on solving the issue fairly, not winning. `[R]`

10. **Plans are cancelled at the last minute. What could genuinely rescue the mood?**
    - A. They create a better night on the spot. `[V]`
    - B. They send one intriguing suggestion and let anticipation build. `[I-aesthetic]`
    - C. “Pack light. Trust me.” `[N]`
    - D. An unexpectedly honest private conversation. `[I-depth]`

11. **What most makes you feel chosen?**
    - A. Consistent reassurance and affection. `[W]`
    - B. Being deliberately included in the future they are building. `[A]`
    - C. Equal effort without having to request it. `[R]`
    - D. Access to a side of them almost nobody sees. `[I-aesthetic]`

12. **The relationship is becoming routine. What brings desire back fastest?**
    - A. A shameless night out and obvious flirting. `[V]`
    - B. A conversation neither of you has ever had before. `[C]`
    - C. Learning or doing something new together. `[N]`
    - D. Time alone that restores longing and depth. `[I-depth]`

13. **A friend says, “I know exactly who to introduce you to.” What description wins?**
    - A. “Mature, kind and genuinely ready.” `[R]`
    - B. “Warm—the sort of person who makes people feel at home.” `[W]`
    - C. “Focused. They are going somewhere.” `[A]`
    - D. “Ridiculously smart, but never boring about it.” `[C]`

14. **Which entrance has the strongest effect on you?**
    - A. Understated, impeccable, impossible not to notice. `[I-aesthetic]`
    - B. Almost silent, but the eye contact is not. `[I-depth]`

### Important item-writing rules

- Keep every option approximately equal in length, vividness and social desirability.
- Rotate type-to-letter placement so no type becomes “the A answer.”
- Do not repeatedly contrast a healthy option with three obvious red flags.
- Use situations rather than abstract adjectives.
- Avoid gendered pronouns in the core version.
- Randomize answer order when technically feasible, while preserving analytics.
- Include at least two reverse or tension items that distinguish attraction from judgment.
- Never lift proprietary inventory items verbatim. The prototype above is original product copy.

## 6. Scoring system

### 6.1 MVP scoring before calibration

Each answer has:

- a loading vector across the seven candidate motives and the two intrigue facets;
- one primary motive and zero to two secondary motive weights in the MVP;
- values on the eight interpretive lenses;
- optional pattern flags such as ambiguity-attraction, caretaking, projection or fast ignition.

For candidate motive \(k\), compute an exposure-normalized score. In the simplest MVP, \(\lambda_{i,a_i,k}\) is 1 for an option's primary motive, 0.20–0.35 for a secondary motive and 0 otherwise:

\[
M_k = 100 \times \frac{\sum_i w_i \lambda_{i,a_i,k}}{\sum_i w_i \lambda^{max}_{i,k}}
\]

where \(w_i\) is 1.25 for Spark items, 1.00 for Pattern items and 1.15 for Partnership items. Normalization matters because only some motives appear in each question and the intrigue facets receive extra experimental coverage.

Those secondary weights should be set by expert review and then estimated from pilot data, not invented permanently at launch.

For a provisional archetype \(t\), compare the seven-motive vector with its hypothesized centroid \(c_t\):

\[
D_t = \sum_k \alpha_k\left(\frac{M_k-c_{t,k}}{\sigma_k}\right)^2
\]

Convert centroid distances to provisional probabilities with a softmax:

\[
P_t = \frac{e^{-D_t/\tau}}{\sum_j e^{-D_j/\tau}}
\]

The temperature τ controls how decisive the result appears. Start conservatively and tune it against validation data. Do not display “87% accurate.” Display the motive profile plus a primary and secondary archetype. Before centroids are calibrated, use transparent hand-authored prototypes and label the test beta.

### 6.2 Result decision rules

- **Clear primary:** first archetype probability exceeds the second by a pilot-calibrated margin.
- **Blend:** first and second are close; title as “Quiet Fire with a Brilliant Tease streak.”
- **Split pattern:** Spark winner differs materially from Partnership winner; lead with “Who pulls you in” and “Who works for you.”
- **Low signal:** excessive speed, straight-line letter selection, contradiction across matched items, or too many skipped questions. Offer a retake rather than false precision.
- **No fallback:** Grounded Equal wins only when its evidence is strongest. It is never assigned because nothing else won.

### 6.3 Lens scoring

Each option carries a value of −1, 0 or +1 on relevant lenses. For example, “rare private access” may score toward intrigue, private expression and slow burn; “a spontaneous plan” toward novelty, fast burn and autonomy. Compute each lens as a weighted mean of only the items that measure it, then transform to 0–100.

Do not derive attachment language from ordinary attraction items alone. Include four carefully reviewed pattern scenarios—reassurance after delayed replies, response to rapid closeness, conflict pursuit/withdrawal and comfort asking for support. Report broad tendencies only.

### 6.4 Post-validation scoring

After sufficient data exist, replace hand weights with a latent-variable model. Forced-choice answers scored by simple totals can become ipsative: high scores on one dimension mechanically force lower scores elsewhere. Thurstonian item-response theory was developed to recover normative latent scores from multidimensional forced-choice data and reduce this problem.[^16]

The mature pipeline should:

1. fit an exploratory model to identify the actual motive structure;
2. confirm it on a held-out sample;
3. estimate item discrimination and thresholds;
4. remove weak, redundant or demographically biased items;
5. compare five- through nine-factor models for the underlying motives;
6. derive empirically observed profile centroids with latent-profile analysis, comparing approximately six through twelve profile solutions;
7. retain the eight branded names only if the data support eight useful, stable regions;
8. calibrate confidence from posterior uncertainty, not score theatrics.

## 7. How to generate readings that feel genuinely personal

The result should be assembled from evidence-bearing modules, not one static paragraph per type.

### Result composition

| Module | Source | Example output |
|---|---|---|
| Headline Spec | primary motive | “Your Spec is Quiet Fire.” |
| Secondary influence | second motive | “But conversation is the lockpick: Brilliant Tease is close behind.” |
| Core pull | top Spark items | “You notice control, private warmth and what is implied rather than announced.” |
| Safety need | safety/attachment lens | “Once invested, however, you need more clarity than your initial attractions tend to offer.” |
| Dating loop | conditional rule | “Ambiguity may keep someone in your mind longer than their behavior has earned.” |
| Strength | primary + adaptive lens | “You read subtle people well and do not require constant performance.” |
| Blind spot | primary + risk flag | “Opacity can borrow the appearance of depth.” |
| Long-term fit | Partnership winner + values | “Look for contained intensity with direct communication and reliable repair.” |
| Growth prompt | highest tension | “Judge mystery by whether it becomes knowledge.” |
| Confidence | score gap/quality | “Strong match” or “A close two-type blend.” |

### Conditional dating-history rules

These statements should never be attached to everyone with a type. Trigger them only when multiple answers converge.

| If the answer pattern shows | The reading may say |
|---|---|
| high intrigue + high reassurance sensitivity | “Hot-and-cold attention may have occupied more mental space than steady interest.” |
| high safety + high caretaking + low boundaries | “You may have become the emotional manager of relationships that felt unequal.” |
| high vitality + fast burn + low consistency threshold | “Your best beginnings may have outperformed their follow-through.” |
| high agency + high approval sensitivity | “Being chosen by an impressive person may sometimes feel like proof of your own value.” |
| high mental attunement + space protection | “Banter may have made closeness possible while also helping you avoid direct vulnerability.” |
| high aesthetics + high projection | “You may complete incomplete people with your imagination.” |
| high novelty + low routine tolerance | “Familiarity may register as fading chemistry before the relationship has learned renewal.” |
| high partnership + low expressed desire | “You may build relationships that function well but need more deliberate erotic and playful energy.” |

Every strong claim should have a visible qualifier: “your answers suggest,” “you may recognize,” “when attraction becomes uncertain,” or “if this pattern is familiar.” Specificity should come from conditional evidence, not pretending uncertainty does not exist.

### The “person to marry” section

Do not output “Marry a Grounded Equal.” Output a **behavioral partner brief**:

> The person most likely to suit you long-term is emotionally direct without being invasive, self-possessed without becoming unavailable, and consistent enough that privacy never turns into confusion. You need depth—but you also need evidence.

This is more useful and more defensible. It incorporates relationship processes that actually matter: responsiveness, commitment, appreciation, conflict behavior, reciprocity and shared values.

## 8. Validation roadmap

Content validity is not established by a clever scoring spreadsheet. Measurement guidance emphasizes relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility, and warns that reliability alone cannot prove an instrument measures the right construct.[^17]

### Phase 0: construct and expert review

- Recruit one psychometrician and two researchers or licensed practitioners with relationship-science expertise.
- Define every motive and lens in one paragraph, including exclusions.
- Have experts independently map every answer option to constructs.
- Revise items with poor agreement.
- Review claims for inclusivity, stigma, trauma language and cultural assumptions.

### Phase 1: cognitive interviews

- Interview 24–40 adults diverse in gender, orientation, age, culture and relationship structure.
- Ask participants to think aloud while answering.
- Check what each option means to them, which choices feel missing, and whether “mystery,” “ambition,” “safety” and “freedom” carry unintended moral judgments.
- Time the quiz on ordinary phones and slow connections.

### Phase 2: pilot study

- Target at least 600 respondents for the first stable calibration; more is preferable for forced-choice IRT and subgroup analysis.
- Split data into development and hold-out samples.
- Collect independent criterion measures: concise ideal-partner ratings, original attachment indicators, relationship intent, recent-partner trait ratings and one behavioral choice task.
- Do not use “Did this feel accurate?” as the main validity metric; the Barnum effect makes that inadequate.

### Phase 3: psychometric analysis

- Examine item choice frequency, discrimination, local dependence and missingness.
- Test structural validity with exploratory and confirmatory models, explicitly comparing five-, six-, seven-, eight- and nine-factor solutions.
- For the public-facing taxonomy, compare latent-profile solutions rather than demanding eight. Consider statistical fit, entropy, minimum class size, interpretability and replication in the hold-out sample together; do not choose on one fit statistic alone.
- Estimate omega or model-based reliability for each continuous motive.
- Assess two- to four-week test–retest stability in at least 150 participants.
- Test measurement invariance and differential item functioning across major gender, orientation, age, region and language groups.
- Compare declared preference scores with vignette choices and remembered partner patterns.

### Phase 4: behavioral validation

The strongest test is not self-recognition but prediction. With explicit consent, examine whether scores predict:

- which fictional profiles users choose in a standardized task;
- attributes of people they actually express interest in;
- whether their Spark and Partnership scores predict different choices;
- follow-up satisfaction with real interactions;
- repeat-test stability without becoming insensitive to genuine life changes.

Do not train the scoring model on engagement alone. A result that maximizes sharing may reward flattering language rather than accuracy.

### Release standards

Before using “psychological assessment” publicly, Udala should be able to document:

- construct definitions and item provenance;
- expert review and cognitive interviews;
- reliability with uncertainty intervals;
- structural and criterion validity;
- subgroup fairness checks;
- version numbers and change logs;
- plain-language limitations.

Until then, use “research-informed attraction quiz” or “playful psychology-based reading.”

## 9. Product and ethical requirements

### Honest framing

Recommended result disclaimer:

> This is a research-informed reflection on your attraction preferences, not a diagnosis or a prediction of destiny. People and relationships change with context. Use the reading as a prompt to notice patterns—not as a reason to ignore what someone consistently shows you.

### Data minimization

Relationship intent, sexual attitudes, orientation and birth date can be sensitive. Collect only what changes the result; explain why; separate optional from required fields; allow deletion; and do not expose a person's result or answers to other users by default. Obtain legal review for every market in which the quiz is offered.

### Safety boundaries

- Never interpret abuse, coercion, jealousy or surveillance as a romantic “type.”
- Do not tell someone they attract narcissists, have trauma, fear abandonment or are incapable of commitment unless a qualified clinical process supports it—which this quiz cannot.
- Do not rank types as healthier, hotter, more feminine, more masculine or more marriageable.
- Do not infer sexual orientation from attraction answers.
- Never use race, tribe, religion, disability or other protected identity as a hidden compatibility weight.
- If the quiz later becomes a matching tool, obtain separate consent and validate matching outcomes independently.

## 10. Recommended result-page structure

The page should reveal information progressively:

1. **Your Spec:** name, one-line hook and primary visual.
2. **Why this pulls you in:** three specific attraction signals drawn from answers.
3. **The twist:** secondary Spec or Spark–Partnership split.
4. **What it says about you:** needs, perceptual habits and relationship values.
5. **Your likely dating loop:** only a triggered conditional module.
6. **Your strength in love.**
7. **Your blind spot.**
8. **Who tends to work for you:** behavioral long-term partner brief.
9. **One experiment:** a practical question or behavior to try on the next date.
10. **Share card:** headline type only; private vulnerabilities never placed on the share image.

Example opening:

> **Your Spec: Quiet Fire**  
> You are drawn to people whose intensity is contained, not advertised. You notice the pause before the answer, the private warmth, the feeling that access means something.  
>  
> **The twist:** Brilliant Tease is close behind. Silence catches your attention, but a mind that can surprise yours is what keeps it.

Example tension module:

> **Who pulls you in versus who lets you thrive**  
> Your Spark score leans toward intrigue; your Partnership score leans toward clarity. That does not mean you should date someone boring. It means the strongest fit gives you layers to discover without making basic interest difficult to verify.

## 12. Final recommendations

1. **Do not lock the product to eight.** Begin with seven candidate continuous motives and let pilot data determine dimensionality.
2. **Treat the current eight names as provisional archetypes.** Keep, merge, split or replace them according to profile stability and narrative distinctness.
3. **Remove the fallback rule.** Grounded Equal must be earned like every other result.
4. **Separate Spark, Pattern and Partnership.** This is the core insight that will make the quiz feel revealing rather than generic.
5. **Return a primary plus secondary Spec.** Most users will not be psychologically well represented by one label.
6. **Use modular conditional interpretation.** The dating-history feeling should come from combinations of answers, not stock copy.
7. **Keep zodiac separate and optional.** It can add delight but should contribute no scientific score.
8. **Launch as a beta reading, not a validated test.** State that it is research-informed while calibration is underway.
9. **Validate against choices and relationship patterns.** “Users said it felt accurate” is not enough.
10. **Prefer behavioral spouse guidance over type matching.** Tell users what qualities and interaction patterns suit them.
11. **Plan for 20 scored questions.** Four minutes remains realistic and gives the model more signal than a 10-question quiz.

The central product promise can remain playful: **“We can guess your Spec.”** The result should then earn trust by making a more intelligent distinction: *the person who activates your attraction is not always the same person who satisfies your deeper relationship needs—and your answers reveal both.*
